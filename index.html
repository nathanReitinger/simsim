How it works

Many definitions of “the same”

A file hash asks whether two files are bit-for-bit identical. A perceptual hash asks whether they would look identical at thumbnail size. SSIM asks whether the pixels line up. SSCD asks whether one is an edited copy of the other. CLIP asks whether they depict the same kind of thing. Each test answers a different question — the disagreements are the interesting part.

Reading the score

Like a malware scanner, the headline counts how many tests flag the pair: Identical or Match count as flags, Partial is shown separately. Thresholds come from the original papers where they exist (SSCD, PDQ, Somepalli et al.) and are labelled as heuristics elsewhere.

Similarity is not infringement

These numbers measure resemblance between pixels or learned features. Whether a resemblance is legally significant — originality, protectable expression, substantial similarity, fair use — is a different question that no metric answers. Treat the scores as evidence to interpret, not conclusions.

Private by design

Images are decoded and analysed on your device with WebAssembly (ONNX Runtime, OpenCV). The neural models are downloaded once (≈ 200 MB in total, configurable in Settings) and cached by your browser.

Built for teaching computer science & law. Models: SSCD (Meta AI, MIT), DINOv2 (Meta AI, Apache-2.0), D-FINE (Apache-2.0), CLIP (OpenAI, MIT), LPIPS (BSD-2). Libraries: ONNX Runtime Web (MIT), OpenCV.js (Apache-2.0), exifr (MIT). Sample photos: NASA, SpaceX (public domain), Stefan van der Walt and Rachel Michetti (CC0), via scikit-image.

Settings

Neural tests need model files. Each is downloaded the first time it is used and then cached by your browser. Turn off the large ones on slow connections.
